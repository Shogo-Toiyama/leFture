**1. TOPICS & KEYWORDS (`topics`):**

- Partition the transcript into 4 to 6 broad, student-friendly learning chapters.
- For each ACADEMIC topic, extract 3 to 6 core academic concepts (`keywords`).
- If the professor returns to an earlier concept for a sustained section, create or continue a chapter that reflects that resumed concept rather than forcing it into an unrelated chapter.
- **Language Rule (CRITICAL):** `topics[].title` MUST be written in the specified output language per `OUTPUT LANGUAGE` above (e.g., in Japanese). Do NOT leave topic titles in English/transcript language. For `keywords`, write them in the output language, and include the original transcript term in parentheses when instructed by the bilingual rule.

**Topic Types:**
- `ACADEMIC`: substantive lecture content.
- `LOGISTICS`: use ONLY if there is a large, continuous, sustained block discussing syllabus, grading, or course administration.
- `OFF_TOPIC`: use ONLY for a sustained non-academic stretch longer than 15 sentences (e.g., an extended tangent, a long break, unrelated conversation). Do not create an OFF_TOPIC segment for brief remarks — see Content Filtering Rule.

**Content Filtering Rule:**
- Pure filler, jokes, brief administrative remarks, and short tangents of 15 sentences or fewer must be silently absorbed into the SID range of the nearer adjacent ACADEMIC or LOGISTICS topic. They must NOT influence that topic's title, keywords, or summary content — they simply extend its SID range.
- Only stretches exceeding 15 sentences become their own OFF_TOPIC segment.
- This absorption rule is what keeps coverage continuous without inflating keyword lists with irrelevant content — do not treat "ignore filler" as license to leave gaps in SID coverage.

**Continuity Rule (CRITICAL):**
- The sequence of topics must be strictly chronological and non-overlapping.
- Gaps are not allowed: `next_topic.start_sid` must equal the SID immediately following `previous_topic.end_sid`. Every sentence in the transcript must belong to exactly one topic's range (ACADEMIC, LOGISTICS, or OFF_TOPIC).
- The first topic must start at or near `s000001`. The last topic must end at or near the final sentence of the transcript.

**Balance Rule (apply only as a tie-breaker per Stage C, never overriding a genuine conceptual boundary):**
- Let N = number of transcript sentences covered by ACADEMIC + LOGISTICS topics, and K = number of those topics.
- Target length per topic ≈ N / K.
- Each topic should generally contain between 70% and 140% of the target length.
- The longest topic should be no more than 1.8× the length of the shortest topic.
- No single topic should contain more than 30% of the covered transcript.
- If a genuine conceptual boundary makes these ratios impossible to satisfy, the conceptual boundary wins — but flag this only by ensuring Stage E validation is aware balance was relaxed for a stated reason, not by silently ignoring it.

**Keyword Constraints (CRITICAL):**
- Focus strictly on theoretical and architectural concepts essential for deep understanding.
- Do NOT include analogies, anecdotal terms, or specific examples used for illustration, even if frequently mentioned.

**Topic Title Examples:**
- Good (broad learning chapters in the requested output language): "Zero Trust Architecture Fundamentals" (or translated into the output language like "ゼロトラストアーキテクチャの基礎"), "Privacy-Preserving Security Monitoring" (or "プライバシー保護型セキュリティ監視")
- Bad (too narrow): "Micro-segmentation", "False Positives", "RELRO"

**4. FUN FACT TOPIC SELECTION (`fun_fact_selection`):**

This is a selection task only. Do not write any explanatory text, angle, or pitch — that happens in a separate step later.

- Review the `<STUDENT_PROFILE>` and identify the ONE topic (by `idx`) from the `topics` list above whose subject matter is most likely to genuinely excite this specific student, based on their stated interests or goals.
- From that topic's `keywords`, select the single existing keyword that is the most concrete, specific anchor point for a future personalized explanation (`concept_focus`). Do not invent a new term — choose one already listed in that topic's keywords.
- Base this purely on topical fit between the lecture content and the student's interests. Do not attempt to judge how "exciting" a written explanation would be — that judgment happens downstream with more context.
- Write `concept_intro_line`: exactly ONE sentence, drawn from the actual transcript content around `concept_focus`, describing how the professor introduced or framed this concept in class (e.g., what problem it was motivated by, what definition or analogy was given). This is reference material for a later step that will remind the student what they learned — it is not a summary of the whole topic, just this one concept's introduction. Keep it factual and neutral, matching the textbook tone used in `summary`, not a promotional or exciting tone.
- **Language Rule:** Write `concept_intro_line` strictly in the specified output language per `OUTPUT LANGUAGE` above. Copy `concept_focus` character-for-character from that topic's `keywords` list.
