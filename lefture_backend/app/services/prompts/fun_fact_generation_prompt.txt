### CRITICAL WRITING RULES

1. **Ban the "Power Grant" Sentence Pattern:**
   Do NOT write sentences claiming "this gives/grants you a [power / edge / weapon / superpower / cheat code / unfair advantage]." Speak directly about the real phenomenon, human drama, or what is actually happening.

2. **The Hook is an Independent, High-Impact Punch (STRICTLY 1–2 SENTENCES):**
   - **Standalone Micro-Story:** The hook MUST be an independent, captivating read on its own. Even without reading the body, the reader must instantly grasp the surprising reality and be hooked.
   - **Zero Classroom Recap in the Hook:** NEVER begin with lecture recap phrases (e.g., ❌ "Remember today's lecture on...", ❌ "In today's class we learned..."). Keep the hook 100% focused on the startling real-world phenomenon, mystery, or mind-blowing fact ("Did you know...?!", "It turns out...").
   - **Strict Sentence Cap:** Exactly 1 or 2 sentences. NEVER write 3 or more sentences for the hook.
   - **Zero Citations in Hook or Title:** Never put citation brackets in `title` or `hook`.

3. **The Body is a Self-Contained, Thrilling Narrative:**
   - **Standalone Reading:** The body must be fully comprehensible even if someone skips the hook entirely. Do not write the body as if it is merely finishing the hook's incomplete sentence.
   - **Exciting 1-Sentence Classroom Connection (Sentence 1 ONLY):** Open the body with exactly ONE exciting, brief sentence that ties the classroom concept (`<CONCEPT_FOCUS>`) to this real-world story. This is NOT a boring lecture review or exam prep—it exists to make the student feel "Wow, what I learned today is actually incredible!"
   - **Jump Directly into the Story:** After that single opening sentence, immediately dive into the `named_instance`, the concrete struggle/mechanism, and how it works.
   - **Close with a Punchline:** End with a memorable, punchy takeaway that leaves the student inspired and eager for the next lecture.

4. **Evidence Citations in Body Using Double Brackets (`⟦#⟧`):**
   - Whenever you incorporate specific facts, turning points, surprising numbers, or mechanisms from `<WEB_SEARCH_RESULTS>`, immediately append the citation number in double square brackets: `⟦1⟧` or `⟦2⟧`.
   - **At Most 1 Citation Per Sentence (Strict Limit):** Never place more than ONE citation in a single sentence. Avoid citing multiple sources (e.g. `⟦1, 2⟧`) or scattering multiple citation brackets across the same sentence. Keep citations subtle and unobtrusive.
   - **No Fake Citations:** Cite ONLY the numbers that appear in `<WEB_SEARCH_RESULTS>` and genuinely support that fact. Do NOT invent citation numbers or claim you used sources that you did not actually rely on.
   - Do NOT output URLs anywhere. The backend will automatically map your `⟦#⟧` tags back to the exact source URLs.

5. **Anchor Minor Proper Nouns with Context (Crucial for Readability):**
   Unless a proper noun is globally famous (e.g., Apple, NASA, Google), never drop unfamiliar company names, video games, historical figures, or specific tools without a short 2–4 word tag providing immediate context so the reader never feels lost.
   - ❌ Bad: `When id Software developed the Quake engine, John Carmack faced a problem...`
   - ⭕️ Good: `When the developers behind the legendary 90s video game Quake (id Software) tried to render 3D lighting, their lead programmer John Carmack hit a wall...`
   - ❌ Bad: `The Danziger 2011 study examined...`
   - ⭕️ Good: `A landmark judicial psychology study analyzing over 1,000 Israeli parole decisions discovered...`

6. **Eliminate Niche Jargon & In-Group Slang:**
   Do not clutter the narrative with unnecessary technical API names or gamer/in-group slang (e.g., avoid `OpenGL rendering pipeline`, `frag`, `zero-overhead abstraction`) unless strictly required. Translate them into universally relatable, vivid language (e.g., `real-time 3D lighting`, `smooth motion`, `hyper-realistic explosions`).
