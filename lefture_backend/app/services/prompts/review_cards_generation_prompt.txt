### TOPIC EVALUATION (The Mapping & Synthesis Engine)
Before generating any cards, you MUST evaluate the transcript segment. This acts as both your "Semantic Zoom" data for the Course Map and your Chain-of-Thought for card generation. Follow this exact sequence:

1. **Filter out noise:** If this topic segment is mostly administrative fluff without testable concepts (e.g., course logistics, chitchat, etc), set `"is_academic_content": false` and return empty arrays (set "micro_topics": [] and "review_cards": []).
2. **Deconstruct the Flow (`micro_topics`):** Break down the chronological flow of this segment into exactly 3 to 5 logical "Micro-Topics". 
   - Each must have a `title` (Max 5 words), a `summary` (1 concise sentence), and the exact `start_sid` and `end_sid` for that specific micro-topic boundary ( **non-overlapping** ).
3. **Card Mapping Strategy (`card_mapping_strategy`):** Formulate a ruthless editorial strategy to fit this entire segment into just 4 mobile-friendly Review Cards ("Short, Fun, Solid"). 
   - **Identify the Climax:** Which part did the professor spend the most time/energy building up to? 
   - **Be Ruthless (The Mobile Constraint):** You CANNOT fit every detail, proof, lengthy code snippet, or tedious step. Explicitly state what dense materials you will intentionally OMIT and relegate to the "Next Action" card to keep cognitive load low.
   - **Combine & Assign:** Explain how you will map the `micro_topics` into the 4 cards (e.g., "Combine Micro-topics 2 and 3 for the 'Core Why', use Micro-topic 4 for the 'Gotcha', and drop the mathematical proof from Micro-topic 5 into 'Next Action'").
4. **Synthesize the Core Narrative (`core_concept_identified`):** Based on your strategy above, define the absolute central theme of this segment in a punchy phrase (Max 5-7 words). This is the "North Star" for your cards.

### CARD TYPES & CRITERIA
You will generate a strictly sequenced set of 4 cards per core mental model (Card 1->2->3->4). Do NOT try to evenly summarize the whole lecture. Focus entirely on finding the professor's "Climax" (where they spent the most time, used the most emphasis, or explained the most complex algorithm) and unpack it deeply.

1. **"hook" (Hook & Analogy)**
   - Goal: Drop cognitive load to zero. Give an intuitive "Aha!" moment.
   - Content: A highly relatable everyday analogy or a punchy one-liner defining the concept. Do not explain the mechanism here.

2. **"core_why" (The Core "Why" & Big Picture)**
   - Goal: The absolute heart of the lecture. Plant the central concept firmly in the student's mind for long-term retention, optimized for a quick mobile glance.
   - Content: Explain the fundamental "engine" of the topic—*how* it works conceptually or *why* it exists. Use code/math blocks if applicable.
   - STRICT RULE: Do NOT write exhaustive, step-by-step algorithms or dense technical manuals here. (The student has separate, comprehensive "Detail Notes" for that). Keep it punchy, bite-sized, and highly impactful. Use 2-3 short blocks to deliver the big picture clearly and enthusiastically.

3. **"gotcha" (The Crux & Pro-Tips)**
   - Goal: Elevate understanding from "I know what it is" to "I know exactly why it works this way." Highlight the professor's absolute core message.
   - Content: The exact mechanism, "Secret Sauce", or critical nuance the professor yelled "Pay attention to this!" about. This can include essential hints, deep-dives into how the gears turn, or "Aha!" insights that make the whole concept click. If a test pitfall was mentioned, include it, but prioritize deep fundamental understanding over simple warnings.
   - Special Rule: Use a `callout` block to visually highlight this "Aha!" moment or Professor's Pro-Tip.

4. **"next_action" (Next Action & The Uncovered)**
   - Goal: Show the limits of these cards and guide future study.
   - Content: A bulleted list of complex terms/mechanisms mentioned in the transcript but intentionally omitted from Cards 1-3. Do not explain them, just list them and urge the student to study themselves.  Briefly hint at how this connects to the [Course Topic Map].

### WRITING RULES
- **Tone:** Casual, enthusiastic, and easy to understand (like a top-tier Tech Blog). No dry, academic textbook language.
- **Faithfulness:** Base the core concepts ONLY on what the professor taught. 
- **Citations:** You MUST append evidence to every text/block using `⟦sXXXX⟧` or `⟦sXXXX-sYYYY⟧`.
- **Formatting:** Use inline Markdown `**bold**` to highlight the most critical 1-2 phrases per text block if applicable. 
- **Code/Math:** If the professor speaks pseudo-code or math, you MUST format it into clean, syntactically correct code blocks or LaTeX.

### BLOCK TYPES ALLOWED (`content_blocks`)
Use these specific block types to make the UI rich and engaging. To explain deep concepts without creating a wall of text, stack multiple short blocks sequentially. You MUST strictly adhere to the required JSON fields for each type.

- `paragraph`: Standard text. 
  - Required fields: `"type": "paragraph"`, `"text": "<string>"`
  - STRICT RULE: Max 2-3 sentences per paragraph block. If you need to explain more, start a new paragraph block.
- `quote`: A punchy, massive text callout (Use for the most important takeaway).
  - Required fields: `"type": "quote"`, `"text": "<string>"`
- `callout`: A colored warning/info box (Perfect for Gotchas, strict rules, or Professor's tips).
  - Required fields: `"type": "callout"`, `"alert_type": "<info|warning|error>"`, `"text": "<string>"`
- `code`: Syntactically correct code or math.
  - Required fields: `"type": "code"`, `"language": "<string>"`, `"code_string": "<string>"`
  - Optional field: `"explanation": "<string>"`
- `list`: Bullet points. Perfect for breaking down multi-step processes or algorithms chronologically.
  - Required fields: `"type": "list"`, `"items": ["<string>", "<string>"]` (Array of strings)
